# Playbook

These are working rules to test against each channel's own analytics, not guarantees. Platform behaviour
changes; review the numbers monthly and keep what the data supports.

## Series, not single videos

A series is a fixed shape: same hook pattern, beat lengths, card layout and end line, with a new topic
each time. It makes production cheap (only texts, facts and data change), teaches viewers what to expect,
and gives every video a next video.

- Name the series and number the episodes on screen (step pill or subtitle).
- Each episode ends by naming the next one.
- Plan 5 to 10 episodes at once in `series.json`; the director designs the shape once.

Starter series by pillar:

| Pillar | Series | Shape |
|--------|--------|-------|
| Maths | Proof in 30 seconds | claim, picture builds, the click, formula |
| Maths | The number behind it | everyday quantity, model, computed answer, limits |
| Space | Tonight's orbit | object, real orbit drawn from Kepler's equation, one number to remember |
| Space | Scale check | two sizes or times compared exactly |
| Chemistry | One-drop reactions | setup, tension, the change, the equation |
| Chemistry | Why that colour | observation, species responsible, balanced equation |

## Retention

- 0 to 2 s: the result or the question on screen. No intro.
- Each beat changes one thing; a new label or number appears about every 2 to 4 s.
- Build to one payoff at about 60 to 75 percent of the length; use the remainder for the reason.
- End on a frame that loops well or names the next episode.
- Sample style is text and music only. That suits silent autoplay and translation. Always keep text
  large enough to read on a phone and on screen long enough (the lint estimates reading time).

## Search and discovery

- Research the term first: what students and curious adults type (syllabus words: "titration curve",
  "brown ring test", "Kepler's third law").
- Title pattern from the channel profile; three candidates per video; keep the main term in the first
  40 characters.
- Description: promise and term in the first two lines, then method, sources and chapters.
- Shorts and Reels: 3 to 5 specific hashtags. Long videos: chapters and a pinned comment with a question.
- Thumbnail (long videos): the payoff frame, at most three words, one object.
- Re-cut: the same project renders 9x16, 16x9 and 1x1. Post natively on each platform.

## Calls to action

One per video. In order of preference: watch the next episode; answer a question in the comments; try
something safe and report the number. Subscribe prompts work best after several videos have delivered.

## Hard to copy

Re-uploads are a platform matter (watermark, report). The larger risk is imitation, and the defence is to
own things an imitator must rebuild:

1. **Computed visuals.** Curves, orbits and surfaces drawn from lab scripts. An imitator needs the science
   and the code, not a screen recorder.
2. **The method card.** Every video ends with what was computed, with what model, and what it does not
   show. It builds trust and it is a recognisable signature.
3. **A style system, not a template.** A style pack plus the channel's signature elements (wordmark
   gradient, accent order, a recurring object, a sound logo). Refresh one element each quarter.
4. **Series continuity.** Numbered episodes, recurring formats and call-backs make single stolen videos
   look incomplete.
5. **Speed with accuracy.** The pipeline makes a correct video fast; an imitator has to choose.
6. **Source access.** Videos built from the owner's own documents, labs and data cannot be reproduced
   from the video alone.
7. **A visible wordmark** inside the composition (not a corner that crops off), and project files kept
   private.

Do not answer imitation by imitating: keep other channels' videos as style references only.

## Posting plan for the first 90 days

- Weeks 1 to 2: one channel, one pillar, two series, 5 Shorts a week. Measure 3-second hold and
  completion.
- Weeks 3 to 6: keep the series that holds viewers; add one long video a week built from the best Shorts.
- Weeks 7 to 12: open the second channel with a different tone (see `channels/`), reusing lab scripts and
  components but not scenes or wording.
- Each month: review titles against search terms, drop the weakest series, add one new signature element.

## Rules for honest content

- No number without a fact. No claim the calculation does not support.
- Label assumptions and fiction (especially on the humour channel).
- Safety line on anything a viewer could try. No hazardous procedures presented as home experiments.
- Follow each platform's rules on disclosing synthetic or altered content where they apply.
